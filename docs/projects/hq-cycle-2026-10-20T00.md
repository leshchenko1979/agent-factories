## 7. The peer push, the re-mint, and the conflict resolution that went the wrong way

The cycle committed `760f3dfd` and handed it to the pusher. The pusher returned **`diverged`**: origin/main
was **2 ahead** (`afb45bc6`, `fcd1e367` — the Worker lane's claim for `#473` and its census fix) and HEAD
**1 ahead**. It refused to resolve, which is correct: it never rewrites published history.

**The collision was a row number, not content.** Their `claim #473` had taken **n=3830**, the number my own
`claim #462` had taken. My four rows were **unpublished**, so they carried no identity any peer could
observe, and `docs/instruments/ledger.md` grants an unpublished row a free **re-mint**. They are now
**3831, 3832, 3833, 3834**. No published row moved.

**The mistake worth recording.** To clear the conflict I ran `git checkout --theirs evidence/ledger.jsonl`.
In a rebase, `--theirs` is the commit being **applied** — mine — so the command took my side and **deleted
the peer's published n=3830 from the working file**. Nothing was committed in that state. It was caught by
comparing the working file against `git show origin/main:evidence/ledger.jsonl`: 3830 rows vs the expected
3831, and the peer's row absent. The file was restored from origin's own blob and verified byte-identical
(md5 `4bd4a92c`) before the rebase continued.

The general lesson is the one this factory keeps relearning in new clothes: **a conflict resolution is a
write, and the side you take must be chosen by reading which side is published, never by which flag reads
like "the other one"**. `--ours`/`--theirs` invert their meaning under `rebase`, and the inversion is
silent — the command succeeds and the file parses.

**The `head=` citation moved too.** The close row first cited `fe203185`, which the rebase left
reflog-only. A durable row must cite a **reachable** commit, so the re-minted close cites `daabd898`, the
commit that was HEAD at re-mint.

**Window cost: none.** The rebase ran with `--committer-date-is-author-date`, so the 900 s hold window was
already satisfied and the retry published on its first poll rather than re-arming the clock.
