## 7. Two peer pushes, two re-mints, and the conflict resolution that went the wrong way

The Worker lane was working `#473` in the same window, and it pushed **twice** while this cycle held its
publish. Both times the pusher returned **`diverged`** and refused to resolve, which is correct: it never
rewrites published history, and a divergence cannot become published by waiting.

| Round | Their commits | Their rows | My rows, re-minted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | `afb45bc6`, `fcd1e367` | `claim #473` at **n=3830** | 3831, 3832, 3833, 3834 |
| 2nd | `6bf34c99` | `close #473` **n=3831** + receipt **n=3832** | **3833, 3834, 3835, 3836** |

**The collision was always a row number, never content.** My four rows were **unpublished** each time, so
they carried no identity any peer could observe, and `docs/instruments/ledger.md` grants an unpublished row
a free **re-mint**. That is what made two rebases cost nothing: **no published row moved**, and the Worker
lane's `n=3830`, `n=3831` and `n=3832` are byte-intact.

**The mistake worth recording.** To clear the first conflict I ran `git checkout --theirs
evidence/ledger.jsonl`. In a rebase `--theirs` is the commit being **applied** — mine — so the command took
my side and **deleted the peer's published n=3830 from the working file**. Nothing was committed in that
state. It was caught by comparing the working file against `git show origin/main:evidence/ledger.jsonl`:
3830 rows against the expected 3831, the peer's row absent. The file was restored byte-identical from
origin's own blob. The second divergence was resolved by restoring that blob **explicitly**, no flag.

The lesson: **a conflict resolution is a write, and the side must be chosen by reading which side is
published — never by which flag reads like "the other one"**. `--ours`/`--theirs` **invert** under
`rebase`, silently: the command succeeds, the file parses, and a peer's published row is gone.

**The `head=` citation moved with each rebase.** The close row first cited `fe203185`, then `daabd898`, and
now `4a1da4e7` — each the commit that was HEAD at its re-mint. A durable row must cite a **reachable**
commit, and a rebase leaves the previous citation reflog-only.

**Window cost: none.** Every rebase ran with `--committer-date-is-author-date`, so the 900 s hold window
was already satisfied and the retry published on its first poll rather than re-arming the clock. That flag
is the whole reason two peer pushes cost minutes instead of 30-minute waits.
